## Outlier Handling

Outlier handling is the process of identifying and treating data points that are significantly different from the majority of observations
in a dataset. Outliers can occur due to data entry errors, measurement errors, unusual events, or genuine extreme observations. They can
affect statistical measures such as mean and standard deviation and may negatively impact the performance of some machine learning algorithms.
Therefore, depending on the nature of the data, outliers can be handled using different techniques. Two common techniques are **Trimming**
and **Capping**.




**1. Trimming:** Trimming is the process of completely removing the observations that are identified as outliers from the dataset.
For example, if the valid range of a variable is between 10 and 90 and a value of 100 is considered an outlier, the entire row containing
100 can be removed. Trimming is useful when the outliers are clearly incorrect, such as data-entry or measurement errors. However, removing
too many observations can result in loss of valuable information and can reduce the size of the dataset.





**2. Capping:** Capping is the process of replacing outlier values with a predefined upper or lower limit instead of removing the
entire observation. For example, if the upper limit is 90 and the dataset contains a value of 100, the value 100 will be replaced with 90.
Similarly, values below the lower limit are replaced with the lower limit. Capping allows us to retain the observations while reducing the
influence of extreme values. It is particularly useful when the outliers are genuine observations and we do not want to delete the
corresponding rows.

The limits for detecting outliers are commonly calculated using the **IQR (Interquartile Range) method**, where IQR = Q3 − Q1. The lower 
limit is Q1 − 1.5 × IQR and the upper limit is Q3 + 1.5 × IQR. Values outside these limits are considered potential outliers.

In short, **Trimming removes the complete observation containing the outlier, whereas Capping keeps the observation but replaces the extreme 
value with an acceptable boundary value.**
